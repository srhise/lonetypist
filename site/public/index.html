FADE IN:

Int. your desk - 2:47 a.m.

A WRITER sits before a word processor with four toolbars, a sidebar, a word-count goal and a small red badge that keeps coming back.

Somewhere in the middle of all that, a cursor blinks. The page is the smallest thing on the screen.

Writer

(to no one)

I just want to write.

The Writer opens LONE TYPIST. The screen goes blue. Eighty columns of white text, a status line, a blinking cursor. Nothing else.

One document fills the screen. The menus wait behind a key and show their shortcuts while they teach them. Files are plain text, saved where you put them.

Cut to:

2.

Int. the screenplay - continuous

The Writer types a scene heading. It snaps to capitals. Enter, and the next line is ready for action. A name on its own line becomes a character cue; Enter again, and the dialogue tucks in beneath it.

Tab turns a blank line into a character, a transition or a scene heading. Names the script already knows finish themselves.

Writer

It's formatting itself.

The screen shows the page exactly as it will print: the same indents, the same line breaks, the same page breaks, with (MORE) and (CONT'D) where they belong. Go to Scene jumps anywhere in the script, and the page count on the status line is the page count on paper.

Cut to:

3.

Int. the printer - morning

The Writer prints. Out comes a manuscript that looks typed, not printed: Courier 10 Pitch on a ten-to-the-inch grid, every letter struck a little harder or softer than the one before, the ink sitting in the paper.

Three ribbons. Fresh, for new-ribbon black. Worn, for a draft that has been through some things. Clean, crisp and even, for contests and agents.

The Writer holds the page up to the light.

Writer

(beat)

Huh.

4.

Int. the same desk - that night

The Writer turns on Typewriter Sounds. Every key strikes like a manual typewriter, recorded from the real machine. The carriage returns. Near the margin, a bell.

Ext. everywhere else - day

Everything stays a plain text file. Screenplays are Fountain, which other screenwriting apps open. There is no account, nothing to sync, and the app never connects to the internet. What you write stays on your Mac.

Writer (V.O.)

One document. One screen.

Fade out.

THE END
